<div class="stage">
<h2><span data-lang="en">Why we built it</span><span data-lang="zh">起因</span></h2>

<div data-lang="en">
<p>The brief was L'Oréal's "beauty for all" strategy reaching Chinese university students, 18 to 25. The default execution for that brief is product marketing with campus ambassadors, which every competitor also runs and which this audience discounts on sight.</p>
<p>Audience research pointed somewhere else. Gen Z rewards brands that read as sincere and socially responsible, and penalises anything that reads as utilitarian marketing. Emotional pressure, from coursework, job hunting and social comparison, is the thing this cohort actually talks about, and the healing economy had already made it a commercial topic rather than a taboo one.</p>
<p>So the campaign was positioned on a claim that is harder to sell internally: beauty is not only external, and a beauty brand can credibly say so only if it stops selling product while saying it.</p>
</div>

<div data-lang="zh">
<p>命题是欧莱雅"美之道"战略如何触达18至25岁的中国大学生。这个命题的默认解法是产品营销加校园大使，每个竞品都在做，而这一受众群体看一眼就会打折扣。</p>
<p>受众研究指向了别处。Z 世代会奖励读起来真诚、有社会责任感的品牌，也会惩罚任何读起来功利的营销。学业、就业与社交比较带来的情绪压力，是这一代人真正在讨论的东西，而疗愈经济的兴起已经让它从一个禁忌话题变成了一个商业话题。</p>
<p>因此提案立在一个对内更难卖的主张上：美不只关乎外在。而一个美妆品牌要让这句话可信，唯一的办法是在说这句话的时候停止卖货。</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="stage">
<h2><span data-lang="en">My approach</span><span data-lang="zh">思路</span></h2>

<div data-lang="en">
<p><strong>Content, platform, then co-creation, in that order.</strong> Four activities across three months, each one handing the audience to the next at a deeper level of participation. Watching costs nothing, listening costs half an hour, showing up costs an afternoon, and creating something costs putting yourself in it. Asking for the last one first is how campus campaigns die.</p>
</div>

<div data-lang="zh">
<p><strong>内容打动、平台渗透、场域共创，顺序不可颠倒。</strong>三个月四场活动，每一场把受众交给下一场时，参与深度都更进一层。看短片不需要成本，听播客需要半小时，到场需要一个下午，而创作需要把自己放进去。上来就要最后一层，是校园活动最常见的死法。</p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
  <p class="label"><span data-lang="en">The decision the structure rests on</span><span data-lang="zh">整个结构立在这个决定上</span></p>
  <p><span data-lang="en">Only the first podcast episode has a fixed topic. <strong>The remaining four are chosen from what students write in the short film's comments.</strong> The campaign therefore cannot decide in advance which emotions matter, which is the point: a brand announcing what young people are anxious about is the failure mode this whole positioning exists to avoid.</span><span data-lang="zh">只有第一期播客的选题是定死的。<strong>后四期从短片评论区里学生自己写下的内容中选出。</strong>这意味着活动无法预先决定哪些情绪重要，而这正是设计意图：由品牌来宣布年轻人在焦虑什么，恰恰是这套定位要规避的失败形态。</span></p>
</div>

<div data-lang="en">
<p><strong>Brand restraint written in as a rule, not left to judgement.</strong> The risk plan states that the campaign keeps brand presence but avoids direct product promotion. A campaign about not being sold to, that sells, discredits itself and the brand at once.</p>
<p><strong>Mid-tier media by design, not by budget alone.</strong> The podcast partners were selected on audience fit rather than reach: a Beijing Normal University psychology professor's show at 180k subscribers, a youth-culture interview show at 150k, a stand-up comedian's daily-life show at 220k with 140k average plays. A top-tier general channel would have bought more impressions and less credibility on this specific topic.</p>
<p><strong>Professional backing on the content itself.</strong> Material reviewed with psychology specialists before release, with university psychology departments providing data support. On this subject, an emotionally effective campaign built without that review is a liability rather than an asset.</p>
</div>

<div data-lang="zh">
<p><strong>品牌克制被写成规则，而不是留给临场判断。</strong>风险预案里明确写着：保持品牌露出，但避免对产品的直接推广。一个讲"不要被推销"的活动如果在推销，会同时毁掉活动和品牌。</p>
<p><strong>中腰部媒体是设计选择，不只是预算所迫。</strong>播客合作方按受众契合度而非声量筛选：北师大心理学教授的节目18万订阅，青年文化访谈节目15万，单口喜剧演员的日常节目22万订阅、集均14万播放。头部泛娱乐渠道能买到更多曝光，但在这个具体议题上可信度更低。</p>
<p><strong>内容本身有专业背书。</strong>物料在发布前经心理学专家审阅，高校心理学部提供数据支持。在这个题材上，一个情绪上有效、但没有经过专业审阅的活动，是负债而不是资产。</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="stage">
<h2><span data-lang="en">What it produced</span><span data-lang="zh">成果</span></h2>

<div data-lang="en">
<p><strong>National 1st Prize.</strong> The deliverable was a 25-page report and a campaign deck, submitted as a proposal.</p>
<p>The projections in that report, and they are projections rather than results: 5 million total social impressions, 30 or more KOLs in content co-creation, three topics reaching platform trending lists, 2 million brand touchpoints, 20 to 30 percent lift in brand favourability measured by post-campaign survey, 2 million students reached, 80 percent of surveyed participants agreeing that art helped them release emotion.</p>
<p>Two pieces designed to outlive the three months: a semester-long campus art co-creation programme, and a white paper on art therapy among Chinese university students, positioned to fill a gap in the field rather than to recap the campaign. The second one is the part I would still argue for. A campaign ends; a piece of research the sector cites keeps the brand in the conversation without the brand having to speak.</p>
</div>

<div data-lang="zh">
<p><strong>全国一等奖。</strong>交付物是25页项目报告与一套策划案，性质是提案。</p>
<p>报告中的预期指标，它们是预测而非结果：社交媒体总曝光500万次，30位以上博主参与内容共创，3个话题登上平台热榜，品牌线上触达200万次，活动后调研显示品牌好感度提升20%至30%，覆盖200万大学生受众，回收问卷中80%的参与者认同艺术对情绪释放有帮助。</p>
<p>另有两项设计为超出这三个月的产出：一个为期一学期的校园艺术共创计划，以及一份中国大学生艺术疗愈白皮书，其定位是填补该领域的研究空白，而不是复述这次活动。后者是我现在仍会为之辩护的部分。活动会结束，而一份被行业引用的研究能让品牌持续留在讨论里，且不需要品牌自己开口。</p>
</div>

<div class="stage">
<h2><span data-lang="en">Postmortem</span><span data-lang="zh">事后复盘</span></h2>

<div data-lang="en">
<p><strong>It was never run.</strong> Every number above is a projection made by the team that wrote the proposal, which is the weakest possible source for a forecast. Nothing here has been checked against an outcome, and a competition win is a judgement on the argument, not evidence that the argument was right.</p>
<p><strong>The central tension does not fully resolve.</strong> A beauty company convening a conversation about emotional pressure is, at some level, buying goodwill on a subject where goodwill is cheap to claim and expensive to deserve. The risk plan addresses this with a rule against direct product promotion, and that rule is necessary but not sufficient. The honest position is that the tension is managed rather than removed, and anyone assessing this proposal should press on exactly that point.</p>
<p><strong>The projections are not modelled.</strong> Five million impressions and a 20 to 30 percent favourability lift are round numbers, not outputs of a media plan with rates attached. Grounding them would mean building up from the partner accounts' actual average plays, which the report does list for the podcast partners and does not use.</p>
<p><strong>The design has one real dependency it does not hedge.</strong> Four of five podcast episodes are chosen from the short film's comments, which assumes the film generates enough comment volume, and enough range, to select from. The risk plan covers low turnout for offline events but not a thin comment section, which is the single failure that would collapse the sequencing.</p>
</div>

<div data-lang="zh">
<p><strong>它从未执行。</strong>上述每一个数字都是撰写提案的团队自己做出的预测，这是预测最弱的来源。没有任何一项经过结果检验，而竞赛获奖是对论证的评判，不是论证正确的证据。</p>
<p><strong>核心张力没有被完全化解。</strong>一家美妆公司召集一场关于情绪压力的讨论，在某种程度上是在一个"善意声称起来很便宜、配得上很昂贵"的议题上购买好感。风险预案用一条禁止直接推广产品的规则来处理它，这条规则必要但不充分。诚实的立场是：这个张力被管理了，而不是被消除了，任何评估这份提案的人都应当在这一点上追问。</p>
<p><strong>预期指标没有建模。</strong>500万曝光和20%至30%的好感度提升是整数，不是一份带刊例的媒介计划算出来的结果。要让它们站得住，需要从合作账号的实际集均播放量自下而上推算，而报告里恰恰列出了播客合作方的这些数字，却没有用。</p>
<p><strong>这个设计有一个没有对冲的真实依赖。</strong>五期播客中的四期由短片评论区决定选题，这假设了短片能产生足够体量、也足够多样的评论供以筛选。风险预案覆盖了线下活动参与度不足，却没有覆盖评论区冷清，而后者是唯一能让整条递进链条塌掉的失败。</p>
</div>
